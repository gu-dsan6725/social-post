# Workshop: Write It Where People Can Read It

A 90-minute workshop on writing technical posts for LinkedIn, X and Medium,
open to all students in Georgetown's M.S. in Data Science and Analytics
(DSAN).

This session is about taking one thing you built or debugged this semester
and writing it up where people outside your class can read it: recruiters,
prospective employers, or anyone looking for good technical content. A good
post is still there when you are job hunting, and a set of posts written
over months builds a public record of how you think.

Attendees bring a laptop and a topic they can explain to someone outside
their field. They leave with a draft they can post.

## Run of show

| Time | Part | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 0:00 to 0:30 | 1. How posts work | One idea, structure, openings, formats, what the feeds reward, timing, the image, AI as an editor, posting on a schedule. Then the model posts and a critique round on four flawed posts |
| 0:30 to 1:00 | 2. Write | Attendees draft on their own topic or one from [topics.md](topics.md). They write the first draft themselves |
| 1:00 to 1:30 | 3. Review | Three volunteers put a draft on screen; the room improves it together. Surprise gifts for volunteers |

## Facilitator notes

- Before the session: recheck the timing and feed advice, which changes
  several times a year. Buy the gifts. Test the deck on the room's projector.
- Part 1: the critique slides reveal each problem on a click. Let the room
  call them out first.
- Part 2: leave the template slide on screen. Walk the room and note two or
  three drafts with a clear idea and a number; those make good volunteers.
- Part 3: about eight minutes per draft. Edit live, quote the exact line,
  and let the writer decide what stays.
- Close: ask everyone to send their live link to a classmate, so each post
  gets a thoughtful first comment.
